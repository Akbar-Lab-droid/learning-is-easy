How to Study

Eight methods that actually work

Science-backed ways to learn. Filter by your goal, pick one method, and use it for two weeks before judging it.

Focus

Pomodoro Technique

Study for 25 focused minutes, then take a 5-minute break. After four rounds, take a longer 15–30 minute break. Short sprints beat long, distracted hours.

Try it: 4 rounds = about 2 hours

Memorize

Active Recall

Close the book and test yourself. Trying to remember — even when you fail — strengthens memory far more than re-reading the same page five times.

Try it: flashcards, self-quizzes, blank-page recall
Learn more
Memorize

Spaced Repetition

Review material at increasing intervals — after 1 day, 3 days, a week, a month. Review right before you would forget it, and it sticks for years.

Try it: free app Anki — ankiweb.net
Learn more
Understand

Feynman Technique

Explain a topic in simple words, as if teaching a 12-year-old. Where you get stuck is exactly where you need to study more.

Try it: teach it to a friend — or a rubber duck
Learn more
Read & Write

Cornell Notes

Split your page: notes on the right, questions on the left, a summary at the bottom. Review by covering the notes and answering your own questions.

Try it: one page per lecture
Learn more
Understand

Mind Mapping

Start with the central idea and branch out with keywords, colors, and drawings. Seeing connections helps your brain organize complex topics.

Try it: one map per chapter, before revision
Learn more
Read & Write

SQ3R

Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review. Turn chapter headings into questions first — then read to answer them. Reading with a purpose beats passive reading.

Try it: for every textbook chapter
Learn more
Memorize

Interleaving

Mix topics in one study session instead of doing all of one subject, then all of another. It feels harder — but your exam performance improves.

Try it: alternate 2–3 subjects per session
